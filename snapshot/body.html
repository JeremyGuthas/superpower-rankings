          How it works

          Every Tuesday, once the week's power rankings have been published, the aggregator finds each outlet's new article, reads all 32 teams out of it, and averages the ranks. A team's Superpower Score is that average — lower is better. Ties break on median rank, then on the team's single best ranking.

          A source only counts when a complete, duplicate-free 1–32 ranking can be read out of its article. Anything short of that is left out of the average for the week and listed above as a miss, so the score is never built on a half-parsed list.

          How this is scored

          The yardstick is the season's final order by win percentage, then point differential — not an official playoff tiebreak, just who won games. Avg error is how many places off the typical team was; correlation is Spearman's rank correlation with that final order.

          The consensus is scored on exactly the same footing as the outlets that feed it, because the interesting question is whether averaging beats its own ingredients.

          Superpower Rankings aggregates publicly published rankings and links back to every source article. All rankings belong to their respective outlets.
